# An Online Cheder — Concept for Discussion

*Prepared for Rabbi Adi Khanukaev. August 2026, draft v0.1. Everything in this document is open to the Rav's guidance — the guardrails and boundaries described here are his to set.*

---

## 1. The Vision

Today the cheder reaches about 30 boys in Atlanta. The number is limited by walls, budget, and geography — not by demand and not by what the Rav has to give. An online cheder removes the ceiling: every Jewish boy, wherever he lives, can receive a real cheder education — davening with a minyan of classmates, Chumash with Rashi, Mishna and Gemara, Chassidus, a chavrusa, a mashpia — in English and Hebrew, on a full daily schedule from morning to evening.

We are not proposing a "budget substitute for families who can't afford day school." We are proposing to build a **top-tier school — one that aims to be the finest Jewish education ever offered**, because technology finally makes it possible: every piece of material is alive and interactive, and every child's program is personal, adapting constantly to his pace and his strengths. The attention that once only a private melamed could give one child can now be given to every child. Affordability is a consequence of the model — not its point.

The goal: from 30 boys to thousands of talmidim.

## 2. Why This Is Possible Now

- **The model is proven in general education.** Alpha School (Texas) compresses full academics into ~2 hours a day on adaptive learning platforms, freeing the rest of the day. Their own key lesson: the platform alone changed little — the results come from the combination of *platform + live mentor + motivation*. People remain the core.
- **The model is proven in values-based education.** American Faith Academy (Christian, $4,900/year, K–12) runs exactly this structure online: self-paced math and language, synchronous classes for everything communal, weekly teacher oversight.
- **The model is proven for a cheder.** The Nigri Shluchim Online School has taught children of shluchim online since 2006 (Gan through 8th grade, ~$4,600/year). Demand is proven over two decades — but it remains a first-generation school: live Zoom classes on a fixed schedule. No one has built the next generation.
- **The funding exists.** Jewish day school tuition runs $10,000–25,000 per child — out of reach for many families, whose children end up in public school. Meanwhile state scholarship programs now cover most or all of a well-priced online program: Florida's Step Up (PEP track) provides roughly $7,000–8,000 per child per year and may be used for online learning programs; Georgia's Promise Scholarship provides up to $6,500. If tuition is set inside the scholarship amount, the school costs an eligible family little or nothing — and the margin funds the live infrastructure below.

## 3. What Stays Sacred: People Raise a Talmid, Not Screens

A cheder lives on chavrusas, rebbeim, a mashpia, a community. A child alone with an iPad is not a cheder — so the schedule is built around a rule: **every learning day, a boy meets live people at least three times.**

1. **Live davening every morning** — the whole cohort together in a video classroom; an interactive siddur supports the reading, but the minyan of classmates is real and daily.
2. **A daily chavrusa** — boys paired by level and time zone, 15–20 minutes on video every day to review and talk through the morning's learning.
3. **Live shiurim with a rebbe** — 2–3 times a week per cohort.
4. **A mashpia for every 10–15 boys** — a fixed mentor, 30 minutes one-on-one with each boy every week, reachable in between.
5. **A local anchor** — monthly in-person gatherings (a farbrengen, a melave malka) at the Chabad House in each boy's city; the shluchim network means nearly every city has one.
6. **Seasonal highlights** — Shabbatons and a summer program, where the friendships that hold a school together are made.

## 4. A Personal Program for Every Talmid

In a physical classroom of 20–30, everyone moves at one pace: the quick boys wait and drift, the struggling boys sink and feel it. Online, this problem disappears:

- Each boy starts with an assessment and receives his own plan per subject — not "3rd grade," but "kriah level 4, Chumash level 2, math level 5."
- A boy moves to the next unit only after mastering the current one (80%+). No one is pushed ahead half-understanding; no one is held back waiting.
- A quick boy is given depth, not busywork — the next sugya, more meforshim. A struggling boy is flagged automatically to his rebbe and mashpia and receives more one-on-one time, not a poor grade.
- The program readjusts every week from real data, and parents receive a clear weekly report.

## 5. Learning That Comes Alive

- **Sugya maps.** For every sugya learned, an interactive map of the chachamim — who says what, who argues with whom, who is quoted by whom. Built and checked by our editors in advance; the boy explores it and then reassembles it himself as an exercise.
- **"Bring it to life."** After finishing a unit, a boy can generate an illustration or a 20–30-second video of what he just learned — strictly within scene templates the Rav approves in advance. Nothing is depicted that should not be depicted; the boundaries are the Rav's, in writing.
- **Chassidus in diagrams.** Interactive charts — sefiros, seder hishtalshelus — that a boy assembles like a puzzle, with every canonical form approved by the Rav.
- **Tickets, a raffle, and a cheder store.** The familiar motivation of every cheder, digitized: boys earn tickets for mastered units, streaks, chavrusa attendance, and davening skills; a monthly raffle is drawn **live** — at the Chabad House gathering or in a full video assembly; tickets can instead be spent in the cheder store on real prizes, or given as tzedakah in the boy's name with the school matching it. (Around davening itself we gamify only skills — fluency, knowing the meaning of the words — never kavanah.)
- **Recess without hefkerus.** Between blocks, a boy plays only from a catalog of approved games, on a locked-down school device. No open internet, no YouTube.

## 6. Guardrails — Set by the Rav

- Nothing generated by AI reaches a child without human review; for this school, the standard of review is the Rav's.
- The Rav sets written guidelines once, and the system enforces them everywhere: what may be depicted, the boundaries of games and prizes, the rules for the AI study assistant (topics it must not touch, when it must refer a boy to an adult, an absolute ban on collecting personal information), daily limits on its use.
- No cameras watching children, no surveillance — oversight comes from mentors and data on learning, not from monitoring software.

## 7. The Economics (Sketch)

- **Tuition target: $3,000–4,500/year** — a third to a fifth of physical day school, in line with Nigri and AFA, and inside the scholarship amounts above, so for eligible families the school can be effectively free.
- **Three sources per family:** the family itself; state scholarship programs; and a scholarship fund for families in states without such programs.
- **Staffing for the first stage:** the Rav (leadership, review, shiurim); 1–2 rebbeim for live classes; mashpiim part-time (1:10–15 — for a bochur, a shlichus in itself); the founding team producing the platform and content. General studies are not produced in-house — they run on proven adaptive platforms.
- **Content honestly:** a full K–8 limudei kodesh program is years of work. The first stage is a hybrid — two or three subjects in our full interactive form, the rest as live classes, digitizing subject after subject.

## 8. What We Ask of the Rav

1. His assessment of this concept — and whether he shares the ambition of section 1.
2. What the cheder teaches today: the curriculum by subject, the teachers, the language of instruction.
3. A few hours a week: reviewing content, giving the live shiurim he chooses to give, and setting the written guidelines of section 6.
4. His name and endorsement, and access to his community — starting with 3–5 volunteer families for a small pilot this fall.
5. Introductions to donors who care about chinuch, for the scholarship fund.
6. A clear list of what is off-limits — fixed in writing before the first child logs in.

## 9. Proposed Path

- **Fall 2026:** a pilot with 3–5 families from the Rav's community — testing the adaptive general-studies stack, the first interactive limudei kodesh units, the daily live rhythm.
- **Winter–Spring 2027:** build-out of content and platform, guidelines and review process running, pricing and scholarship mechanics settled.
- **Elul 2027:** first full wave of enrollment.
